### Source-attributed summary

This summary uses the published excerpt from arXiv (cs.AI). It has not been independently fact-checked by Neural Pulse.

Enterprises increasingly use large language model agents to execute standard operating procedures (SOPs): long, branching, tool-intensive workflows. Yet there is little controlled evidence for choosing among competing agentic architectures. Holding the model, tasks, and tools fixed and varying only the architecture, we compare four designs (a function-calling single agent, a skill-augmented agent, centralized multi-agent orchestration, and a triage swarm; eight arms) across 13 enterprise SOPs, and introduce a skill-definition quality rubric to build matched high and low-quality skill corpora. Three takeaways emerge. (1)~\textbf{There is no universal best architecture; the right choice is SOP-dependent.} On a given SOP, task success ranges from under $10\%$ on the hardest to near $100\%$ on the easiest; no architecture is statistically separable in aggregate, and added structure only plausibly pays off on complex, branching SOPs (a directional, not yet significant, trend).
