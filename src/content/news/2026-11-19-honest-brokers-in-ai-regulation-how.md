### Source-attributed summary

This summary uses the published excerpt from arXiv (cs.AI). It has not been independently fact-checked by Neural Pulse.

The regulation of Artificial Intelligence is susceptible to various forms of capture by industry actors that hold undue influence over the policymaking process, due in part to their information advantage in developing these technologies. In the following paper, we investigate the roles of public commenters in providing evidence to potential regulation in the USA. We build a corpus of 40 potential regulations, and 2,664 public comments submitted by a variety of stakeholders. We computationally process these texts to extract evidence meant to inform regulators, in the form of formal citations and informal references. We find significant differences in the type and amount of evidence supplied: Holding comment length constant, business commenters introduce altogether less evidence, and government, university, and civil society commenters provide significantly more research and legal sources. The publication status of cited research is nearly evenly split, with preprints and peer-reviewed articles being introduced by commenters of all types.
