Intelligent landslide detection is essential for disaster prevention. However, existing models primarily focus on isolated visual tasks, require extensive labeled data, and lack deep analytical and scene-understanding capabilities. To address these limitations, we propose LandslideFM, the first unified foundation model that connects visual perception tasks with contextual interpretation of landslides. Specifically, LandslideFM adapts to multiple visual tasks via a Source-Agreement Graduated Trust semi-supervised learning framework that leverages limited labeled data alongside abundant unlabeled data. Furthermore, predicted landslide regions provide explicit spatial guidance to a Vision-Language Model adapted through parameter-efficient fine-tuning, enabling comprehensive landslide reasoning. To support training and evaluation in this domain, we also present LandslideVL, the first benchmark for intelligent landslide analysis, combining visual imagery and newly introduced textual prompts. Comprehensive experiments across public datasets and LandslideVL demonstrate that LandslideFM achieves superior quantitative and qualitative performance.
