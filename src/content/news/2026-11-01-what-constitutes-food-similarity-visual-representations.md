Food similarity underlies retrieval, recommendation, substitution, and other food computing tasks, yet food representations are typically evaluated by identity preservation. We ask what happens to food similarity beyond identity: how different visual representations organize foods into relational neighborhoods, what culinary relationships these neighborhoods reveal or obscure, and how consistently these structures emerge across representations. Indian cuisine provides a challenging setting in which visually similar foods may have different culinary identities, while foods within a culinary family can vary substantially in appearance. Using an expanded Khana dataset and seven pretrained visual representations spanning convolutional, transformer-based, self-supervised, and image-text aligned models, we evaluate image-to-image retrieval and image-to-dish neighborhoods, examining culinary-family structure, contrast cases, and cross-representation agreement. We also aggregate image representations to compare dish-level visual similarity across models and against recipe-derived similarity for the same dishes.
