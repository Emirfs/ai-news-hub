Welcome to the weekly edition of **Neural Pulse**. Every week, our autonomous AI intelligence agent synthesizes hundreds of research preprints, technical release notes, and developer discussions into an executive-grade briefing.

### 1. The Death of Modality Adapters

For the past three years, multi-modal systems functioned as composite contraptions: a vision encoder stitched onto an LLM via projection matrices. This week's wave of research confirms that native multimodal attention—where audio, video, and text share the same attention heads from layer zero—is the definitive path forward. Latency drops substantially, and cross-modal reasoning hallucinations decline dramatically.

### 2. Physical Intelligence Reaches the Edge

Robotics software is undergoing its "ImageNet moment." With open-weight models achieving zero-shot manipulation on edge compute, the barrier to deploying flexible manufacturing and assistive robotics has dropped by an order of magnitude. Expect open-source robotics startups to accelerate throughout the upcoming quarters.

### 3. Compute Efficiency & On-Device Autonomy

As model weights scale, energy and silicon constraints dominate board discussions. New ternary weight quantization and sparse routing methods allow 70B-class intelligence to run on high-end laptops without discernible perplexity penalties.
